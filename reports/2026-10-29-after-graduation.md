## What it shows

The pre-registered test fails, and not narrowly. Not one of the 48 cells made money on
average in either period. The best discovery cell, `drop60|rise30|1-3h` with a 2x
target, lost 12.6% per trade on the holdout [-16.0% to -9.1%]. In most of its trades the
price broke the low it had bought off before it reached 2x (595 of 815 discovery trades,
1,166 of 1,563 holdout trades). Buying every graduate at one hour and holding three days
lost more (mean -47.3% and -32.2%, win rate 2-3%; coins with no swap after their first hour aren't in it). Waiting for the dump and the bounce
loses less than buying blind, but it still loses.

The two checks agree. The bars match the existing pool-price code on all but 0.6% of the
closes compared. Rerun on closes only, so that no wick inside a minute can trigger
anything, every cell still lost money in both periods.

What this doesn't test:
- V4 chooses coins by their story, and checks that the largest holder holds under 4%.
  Neither can be applied to this history. V4's SWolf and PUMPKIN trades may have been
  good picks, but these rules applied to every coin are not a strategy on their own.
- There are no network costs here, which would make every result worse.
- The pool's constant product is taken from the deposit, so late prices drift a few
  percent low as fees build up.
- The data has holes. Of the 23,526 graduates whose 72 hours fell inside the data's
  range, 16,489 remained once those crossing a hole were left out.

Per the pre-registration, bottom bidding and conviction holds after graduation join the
families that lost. No live test follows from this.
