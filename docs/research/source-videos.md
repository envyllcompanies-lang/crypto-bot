This file lists every rule in the source videos, in the order the creator gives them, followed by the claims to test. It paraphrases the transcripts, with short quotes only where the exact wording matters. Every claim is the creator's, and none has been verified.

## V1 rules, in order

### 1. Universe (terminal filters, set once)

| Column | Filter | Value |
|---|---|---|
| All | Launchpad | Pump.fun only. Other launchpads are "99%" zero-volume or scams, for example honeypots you can't sell |
| New pairs | Min market cap | $7,000, so only tokens with "real people buying" show |
| Final stretch | Max age | 40 minutes |
| Final stretch | Dev holding | ≤ 5% |
| Final stretch | Insiders | ≤ 20% |
| Final stretch | Pro traders | ≥ 10 |
| Final stretch | Min market cap | $8,000 |
| Migrated | (none) | He doesn't trade migrated tokens at all |

The 40-minute cap applies to **Final Stretch**, which is Terminal's "Soon" column of tokens nearing graduation (V2). Combined with the column itself, it selects tokens that got most of the way up the bonding curve within 40 minutes. That is a small, fast-moving group, not "every token under 40 minutes old". V1's Final Stretch examples sit at 87-100% of the curve (see "Dollar levels" below).

### 2. Token type: X communities only

Each token's X link is one of three kinds:

| Kind | His verdict |
|---|---|
| Tweet | Pure speculation; don't trade |
| Profile | "99%" are rugs; worst kind |
| Community | The only kind he trades |

### 3. Checks on every token

He calls these four "muscle memory" checks: holders, holder funding, the community and the chart.

1. **Holder balances.** Look at the top 5 holders below the liquidity pool. Their SOL balances should be varied, like his example of 5, 3, 1, 1 and 4.7. If they are all alike, for example all about 0.1 SOL, it's a bundle.
2. **Holder funding.** Check where each top-5 wallet was funded from (exchange, terminal sign-up wallet and so on) and when. Varied dates, like his example of 6 days, 22 days, 3 years, 7 months and 2 days, are fine. Clustered dates, such as all "1 day ago" or all "25 minutes ago", mean a bundle.
3. **Community.** All three must hold, or you leave:
   - The contract address is in the community's description.
   - There is a pinned post with the contract address and the narrative.
   - Real people are posting, with no random unmoderated links (drainer risk).
4. **Chart (bundle pattern).**
   - The dev launches at about $4-5K, then one big green candle with no sells runs to about $10-14K.
   - Then come small staged sells meant to look human, real buyers come in, and "the bundle nukes it".
   - He says this pattern always means a bundle, provable through checks 1-2. His example's top holders held 0.02, 0.1, 0.03 and 0.1 SOL, almost all funded "25 minutes ago".

The dip method also requires that a token does **not have "a ton of tracked wallets"** in it, meaning wallets that traders track, such as known traders and KOLs. He also expects you to like the narrative ("you just think the narrative is funny").

### 4. Where to look

- Every token in New Pairs.
- The **bottom four** of Final Stretch, sorted by market cap.
- Skip the top two: they are older and higher-cap, so the scalps are smaller.

### 5. Entry: the 40% dip method

- Wait for a **40-50% drop from the all-time high**, then buy. He says buyers wait for that "nuke" to get a low entry, and the token gets "bought right back up".
- Shallow dips don't count. His example of a dip that is too small is "$40K to $30K", which he calls 10%. That is actually 25%. So the cut-off between too shallow and deep enough lies somewhere from 25% to 40%.
- His examples, both from the same coin ("Middle Finger"):
  - $4K launch, run to $10K, a 44% dip, then recovery.
  - Later, an all-time high of $58K, a 45% dip, then "every single time ... another 45%" back up.
- The method applies to **both** New Pairs and Final Stretch, with different sizes (see 7).

### 6. Exit

- **Stop:** sell the whole position the moment price is below your average entry ("be a Jeet"). In effect the stop is your entry price, with zero tolerance.
- **Take profit:** he gives no exact rule. He mentions a bounce of about +45% from the dip low, and says "2x or 50%" is a good result on a small portfolio. A 45% drop followed by a 45% bounce gets back to about 80% of the old high.

### 7. Position size (largest buy per trade)

| Portfolio (SOL) | New pairs | Final stretch |
|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 0.05 | 0.07 |
| 0.5 | 0.1 | 0.2 (transcript says "2") |
| 1 | 0.25 | 0.4 (transcript says "04") |
| 5 | 1 | 1.5 ("adjustable") |

- Under 0.1 SOL, don't trade, because fees eat it.
- For small portfolios, use exactly these sizes, no more and no less.
- At a 1 SOL portfolio, one trade is 25-40% of the account.

### 8. Narrative

He says it can't be taught as rules ("the best teacher ... is time"), and narratives change daily.

## V2 settings that matter for the strategy

V2 is set-up only (wallet, Terminal, layout). These settings bear on the strategy:

- **Filters:** Pump.fun only in every column, and **Mayhem mode hidden**.
- **Final Stretch sort:** by market cap instead of curve progress. This is what "top two" and "bottom four" in V1 refer to.
- **Stats he hides as useless:**
  - Dev bonded count and dev created count. He doesn't use developer history.
  - Dev funded, snipe count, dex boosted and alpha groups/mentions.
  - Top-10-holder entry/exit markers, tracked-wallet buy lines, sniper-trade markers and Mayhem-trade markers.
  - Fresh-wallet buys are optional: "kind of good" to see, but he doesn't use it.

## V3: attention and following traders

V3 is a different creator's beginner guide, not a rule set. What bears on the strategy:

- **Memecoins are an attention bet.** Buy what you think will get more attention: be early to a viral thing, ask whether it's the first of its kind, whether attention is still growing, and who already holds it.
- **Follow top traders.** Follow leaderboard wallets in the FOMO app and get a notification when they trade. He also says "don't copy trade people", because streamers know viewers copy them.
- **Where to look:** the "bonding" tab (tokens about to graduate) and the graduated tab.
- **Rotation:** when a new narrative breaks, traders sell their longer-term coins to chase it, which he calls an entry into those coins.
- **Holds:** every big win he cites is a long hold of a large runner, mostly far past graduation (millions of dollars of market cap).
- **His own record in the video:** a $3K loss on Anom, named traders who round-tripped big gains, and one live $50 trade that made $2.

## V4: narrative scalps, bottom bids after migration, conviction holds

V4 (pasted 28 Oct 2026) is another creator's video advertising a paid group. He claims
about $100K in a month, "all on chain, you can check every trade yourself", but names no
wallet in the transcript, and shows three winning trades. His three strategies:

- **Narrative scalps.** A story breaks on X (he watches a feed of big accounts' posts,
  "if Elon says something funny, that gets tokenized"), a coin launches on it in New
  Pairs, and he is in and out within minutes, out at about 2-3x ("23x" in the
  transcript, almost certainly "2-3x"). Most such coins are back to zero within the
  hour.
- **Bottom bidding good narratives.** He waits until a coin has graduated and dumped,
  lets the early buyers take profit, and buys the bottom once it starts to reverse,
  adding on dips. Example: SWolf, the mascot coin of the STRK altcoin while STRK rose
  300%; bought around $100K market cap after migration, average exit around $500K
  (about 5x). Before buying: largest holder under 4% (he aims for 3.5%), and a second
  figure ("DRO" in the transcript) at 20-28%, under 30%.
- **Conviction holds.** He holds for days a coin whose story he expects to keep growing,
  buys more as it falls (PUMPKIN: from $1M back to $200K, he added) and takes profit on
  the way up. Example: in around $150K, average exit about $1.3-1.56M, peak $4M; he says
  he gave back about $80K of $120K in open profit. His rule: hold only if he can say in
  one sentence why more people will care tomorrow.
- **Risk rules:** decide the exit before buying, never put the whole account in one coin,
  stop after losses (no revenge trading), journal every trade, learn one strategy at a
  time.

**Conflicts with V1.** V1 sells everything at the first price below entry; V4 adds to a
position through an 80% fall. V1 skips migrated coins; two of V4's three strategies
trade only after migration.

## Claims that can't be tested

- "Months where I do not lose, not even in a single day."
- "$50 into $500K", "0.1 SOL into 100 SOL", "8 months straight with this exact same strategy".
- "Works 100% of the time" and "every single time". C1 tests this directly.
- V3's "$30 into $100,000" examples: real on chain but picked from millions of wallets, so they show the tail exists, not how often it comes.
- V4's "$100K last month" and its three example trades: no wallet is named, and three winners chosen after the fact say nothing about how often the method wins.

## Conflicts of interest and risks

- **Paid group:** V4 sells a membership ("the first 50 people get the current price, then it doubles"), a scarcity pitch, and links exchanges and trading apps in its description.
- **Referral income:** V3 promotes the FOMO app with a sign-up link in its description.
- **Referral income:** V2's Terminal link uses his referral code. He earns from viewers' trading volume whether they win or lose.
- **Platform change:** V1 uses Axiom and V2 uses Terminal. Filter names like "insiders" and "pro traders" are each platform's own and may not mean the same thing on both.
- **Key export:** V2 shows exporting the Phantom private key and pasting it into Terminal. That gives a third-party website full control of the wallet. This project's rule stands: keys never leave the owner's `.env`.
